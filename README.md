# swift-motion

Use this package when you render a video frame by frame from SwiftUI views on macOS and need exact timing and a correctly tagged MP4. Describe the animation as a `Motion.Scene` at a `Rate`, draw each state with `Motion.Hosting.Cache` or `Motion.Hosting.Window` into a `Raster<Motion.Pixel>`, and write the frames with `Motion.Encoding`. To film a real view whose own animations must run, host it with `Motion.Hosting.Live`, which steps the process's time one frame at a time through `Motion.Clock`; the executable that does so links the `Motion Clock Shims` product. To set a recorded run of frames, such as a live view's, inside a drawn film, keep it as a `Motion.Take` and show its frames as pictures; to record it once and draw it in later runs, where there may be no screen, write it to a file with `Motion Take File` and read it back a frame at a time. To make a film from pictures of swift-picture rather than views, cut its scenes into a `Motion.Reel` of `Motion Picture`, print proofs of it to look at it without watching it, draw drafts of it at a smaller scale and keep drawn frames for later runs, and give its executable the `Motion Command` command line in one call. To put a window, a terminal's lines, a caption, a headline, a title whose letters arrive one by one, a checklist, a readout, a progress ring, a shockwave, a wall of moving type, a glint or a twinkle on screen in such a film, draw the components of `Motion Graphics` at moments your own timeline makes. `Motion Take File` needs the package's `File` trait, which brings swift-file-system into the graph, as `Command` does; so does `Motion.Encoding.reduced(_:to:within:)`, which measures, copies and moves its files through it, and `Command` turns `File` on. `Motion Picture` needs the package's `Picture` trait, and `Motion Command` its `Command` trait and `Motion Graphics` its `Graphics` trait, each of which turns on `Picture`; with no trait the four libraries are empty. To judge a film, read a movie back as frames with `Motion.Decoding`, set it beside a reference movie or a reel at the same instants with `Motion.Pairs`, lay a stretch of a sound under it with `Motion.Encoding.score(_:with:to:)`, and make it smaller for sharing with `Motion.Encoding.reduced(_:to:as:)` at a preset size or `reduced(_:to:within:)` within a number of bytes; `Motion.Command.main(arguments:)` does each from the command line without a reel. For timecodes alone use swift-smpte-st-12, and for colour signal code points use swift-itu-t-h-273.

## Laws

- Time is a `Rate` from swift-rate, in frames per second, which this package re-exports: an exact fraction, so 29.97 is 30000/1001, with exact frame starts and centers. `Rate.film`, `Rate.ntsc` and `Rate.thirty` name the usual ones. This package declares no rate of its own.
- Sound lines up with picture through two rates: the audio samples in a frame are the audio rate's `index(at:)` at the frame's end less that at its start. Over any run of frames they add up to the exact floor of its duration: five frames at 30000/1001 hold exactly 8008 samples at 48 kHz.
- A frame's timecode is `SMPTE_ST_12.Timecode(frame:at:)` from swift-smpte-st-12, which this package re-exports: drop-frame at 30000/1001 and 60000/1001, and a typed failure at a rate SMPTE ST 12 does not count.
- Counts are `Cardinal` and positions are `Ordinal`, as in swift-raster: widths, heights, strides, bit rates and numbers of frames are counts, and a frame's index and a pixel's x and y are positions. Where AppKit, AVFoundation or the window server takes a signed integer, a value that does not fit is a typed failure or nil, never a trap.
- `Motion.Scene.plan` samples the state at the center of each frame. It lists each distinct state once, in the order first shown, so each is drawn only once, and `shows` gives for each frame the position of its state in `states`.
- `Motion.Render.check(_:render:)` renders every frame it is given twice, in order, and reports the first that differs, with the PSNR between the two, or that it changed size. `check(first:render:)` does so for the frames from zero up to a count.
- `Motion.Render.Comparison` compares two renders position by position, in one pass over each: how many frames both have, how many are the same picture, the differing frame with the lowest PSNR (the first on a tie), the frames whose sizes differ, and how many frames one render has beyond the other's end.
- A frame's picture is a `Raster<Motion.Pixel>`: the `Raster` of swift-raster, which this package re-exports, over `Motion.Pixel`, the BGRA8, premultiplied, sRGB pixel. The pixel type says what the numbers mean, and the molecule's cropping, sampling, resampling, warping, convolution, `map`, `zip` and `reduce` apply. This package declares no `Raster` of its own. A `Motion.Pixel` with a colour channel above its alpha cannot be made. `Raster(width:height:stride:bytes:)` reads bytes in blue, green, red, alpha order at any row stride, from an array or from memory as a `RawSpan` (a pixel buffer, a stored entry), copying them once, drops the padding, and refuses a wrong stride or size, a pixel brighter than its alpha, or sizes whose bytes cannot be counted (`.overflow`) with a `Motion.Pixel.Error`. `bytes` gives the pixels back packed, `width × 4` bytes a row. Two rasters are equal when their sizes and pixels are.
- `psnr(against:)` on a frame's raster compares colour channels only and leaves alpha aside, through the molecule's `psnr(against:peak:_:)`. Equal pictures give infinity, and pictures of different sizes give `nil`.
- `Motion.Encoding` writes H.264 into MP4. The movie's timescale is the rate's numerator in lowest terms, so every frame lasts exactly the denominator in ticks: 1001 of 30000 at 29.97. A rate whose numerator does not fit a timescale fails with `.rate`. By default frames are tagged 1-13-1 at limited range, sRGB carried through the BT.709 matrix, at a bit rate of four bits per pixel per second, which stops at the largest count.
- A signal AVFoundation has no name for, or one at full range, is refused before anything is written, rather than written untagged. A width, height or bit rate AVFoundation cannot be told fails with `.size`, as does a side shorter than `Motion.Encoding.side`, 16 pixels, which AVFoundation's software H.264 encoder, taken where no hardware encoder is free, writes as a movie no decoder reads (measured on macOS 27.2 with the encoder forced to software). A number of frames that ends later than the timescale can say fails with `.length`. All of these fail before any frame is asked for. Frames are asked for by position, in order from zero, and one that is missing or of another size stops the write with `.frame` and its position.
- `Motion.Encoding.Inspection(of:)` reads a movie back from its file: the codec's four-character code, the size, the frames counted by stepping through every sample of the picture's sample table, which reads no sample data and waits on no reader, the length as the exact fraction the file states, the colour signal where it is one this package writes, and the number of sound tracks. `Motion.Encoding.read(_:each:)` decodes every frame in order and hands each over with its position. A file that is no movie, or has no picture, fails with `.reader`.
- `Motion.Encoding.Inspection.rate` is one over the shortest frame duration the file states, exactly, or nil when it states none; for a movie whose frames all last the same, which is every movie this package writes, it is the frame rate.
- `Motion.Decoding` reads a movie back as frames, beside `Motion.Encoding` in the same target: `frames(at:)` gives, for each instant in seconds from the movie's start, the frame shown there, which is the last frame whose presentation starts at or before it, with no tolerance either way, and nil for an instant before zero or from the end of the last frame on. `frames(positions:)` gives the frame shown at each position's center at the inspection's rate, refuses a position at or beyond the frames with `.frame` before anything is decoded, and a movie that states no frame duration with `.reader`. One `AVAssetReader` pass reads the stretch asked for, from a second before its first instant, and only the frames shown at an instant asked for become rasters. The track's preferred transform is not applied.
- `Motion.Encoding.mux(_:_:to:)` joins a picture and a sound without re-encoding either. The sound is cut to the picture's length, and the picture keeps its colour tag, timescale and frame durations.
- `Motion.Encoding.score(_:with:to:)` lays `Motion.Encoding.Cue`s under a movie's picture. The picture is copied sample for sample, so it keeps its codec, colour tag, timescale, frames and their durations. A cue is a stretch of a sound file: from `offset` in the sound, entering at `entry` in the film, until its sound or the picture ends, at `gain` times the sound's level, rising from silence over `rise` and falling to it over `fall`, in straight lines; fades longer together than the stretch are each cut to half of it. The cues are mixed into stereo AAC at 48 kHz, silent before the first enters. Where `mux` copies the sound as it is, `score` decodes it, because gains and fades change the samples. A cue that lays no sound under the picture fails with `.cue` and its position, and a time a `CMTime` cannot hold exactly with `.instant`, before anything is written. A file already at the output fails the write.
- A movie is made smaller for sharing in one way: its picture is decoded and encoded again as H.264 at a smaller size and a bit rate, scaled to that size by AVFoundation's writer, with its frames, their durations, its timescale and its colour tag kept, and its sound copied sample for sample; a movie with more than one sound track fails with `.reader`. Its samples are read and appended on a thread of its own, outside Swift's cooperative pool, as a movie read back or scored is, since a reader's output blocks the thread that waits on it. `Motion.Encoding.reduced(_:to:as:)` does so at a `Motion.Encoding.Preset`: the largest size of the picture's shape within 960 × 540, 1280 × 720 or 1920 × 1080, never larger than its own, each side rounded down to an even number of pixels and at least `Motion.Encoding.side`, at four bits per pixel per second, as `Motion.Encoding` writes by default.
- `Motion.Encoding.reduced(_:to:within:)` makes the best movie whose file is at most a number of bytes. A movie already within the limit is copied as it is. Otherwise sizes of the picture's shape are tried from its own down, each side three quarters of the one before, rounded down to an even number of pixels, while the shorter side is at least 16, and last the size whose shorter side is 16. At each size the bit rate is the bytes the limit leaves the picture (the limit less the sound's sample bytes and an allowance for the file's index of 2 KiB and 16 bytes a frame) over the movie's length, at most four bits per pixel per second; a size at which that is under half a bit per pixel per second is passed over. A movie written over the limit is written again at that size, at most three times in all, its bit rate cut by how far its picture went over and a tenth more, while it stays at half a bit per pixel per second or more. The first movie within the limit is kept, so best means the largest size first and then the most bits the limit allows there, with shape, frames, their durations, rate, colour tag and sound always kept. Sizes are measured on the written file, never predicted. When the smallest size at half a bit per pixel per second is still over, it fails with `.limit`, the limit and the fewest bytes a movie was written in, and leaves no file. A file already at the output fails with `.writer` before anything is written.
- No wait on AVFoundation is without an end either. Each has the hosts' limit, ten seconds on the wall clock: loading what a movie holds (its tracks and their format, timing and length) for an inspection, a read, a score, a reduction or a join; each sample a reader gives; each frame or sample a writer takes, and its finishing; and a join's export moving on, a change in its state or progress, since a whole export lasts as long as its movie needs. The frames a writer is fed are the caller's and have no limit. Past the limit the work fails with `Motion.Encoding.Error.unanswered`, naming what was waited for and the limit. A reader's output, a writer's input and a writer are each called on a serial queue of their own, one call at a time, never on Swift's cooperative pool, since a reader's next sample blocks the thread that asks. A call unanswered at the limit is left behind on its queue, not stopped: AVFoundation forbids `cancelReading()` while a sample is being asked for, and calling it then crashed the process. The movie's asset is told to stop loading instead, which ended a read waiting on the file's bytes at once (the reader then says it completed, which the limit's failure overrides); a decoder that never answers keeps its thread. A load told to stop fails at once, and an export past its limit is cancelled. Measured on macOS 27.2 on Apple silicon.
- Both hosts draw each frame in a fresh hosting view, so no view state carries over from the frame before, and they reuse one window while the size stays the same. `Motion.Hosting.Cache.forget()` and `Motion.Hosting.Window.forget()` make the next frame start from a new window.
- `Motion.Hosting.Cache` draws on the CPU at one pixel per point and needs no permission. Its blur and shadows are the software versions, slightly different from the screen's.
- `Motion.Hosting.Window` draws through the window server like the screen does and needs Screen Recording permission. Only one process should capture at a time. Its animations are frozen, so a spinning view captures the same every time, and the picture comes back pixel for pixel. Sizes must be whole points on the main screen and at least 16 points wide, or the result is nil. Captures take turns, and each one is checked to show its own frame before it is returned.
- `Motion.Clock` is the process's virtual clock. It works through the `Motion Clock Shims` library, which replaces four ways of reading the machine's uptime for every other image of the process: `mach_absolute_time`, `mach_continuous_time`, `mach_approximate_time` and `CACurrentMediaTime`. On macOS 27.2 the system's own `CACurrentMediaTime` already reads the replaced `mach_absolute_time`, so its replacement changes nothing a test can see there. Until a clock is made they pass straight through. Making one freezes all four at that instant, and from then on they move only by `advance`, by exactly the time advanced, however long the wall clock runs. Nothing thaws the clock: it stays virtual until the process ends.
- The shim library must be in the process from launch, because the dynamic linker binds the replacements only then. Link the `Motion Clock Shims` product into the executable, or start the executable with `DYLD_INSERT_LIBRARIES` naming the built library, which the system ignores for hardened and system binaries. Without the library `Motion.Clock()` fails with `.absent`. With a library that was loaded after launch it fails with `.unhooked`. In both cases nothing is frozen. The shim's code built into the executable itself is not replaced either, which is why the product is a dynamic library. A clock therefore needs a process of its own: every thread and every framework in the process reads the same frozen time.
- The clock counts whole nanoseconds from the freeze and never runs backwards. `advance(to:)` takes an instant measured from the freeze, cuts it to whole nanoseconds, and refuses one before `elapsed` with `.backwards` and one past its count with `.beyond`, moving nothing. `advance(to:at:)` goes to the exact start of a frame at a `Rate`, so no error builds up from frame to frame: 30000 frames at 30000/1001 end at exactly 1001 seconds. A second `Motion.Clock()` is a second handle on the same clock.
- What the clock does not reach: the wall clock (`gettimeofday`, `Date`, `CLOCK_REALTIME`), `CLOCK_MONOTONIC`, and other processes including the window server. Waits follow from which clock they read. A run-loop timer (`Timer`) set while the clock is frozen fires only once the clock has been advanced past its fire time, so it is stepped with the frames. A `DispatchQueue` deadline and `Task.sleep` on the system's clocks are waited out by the kernel in real time and fire by the wall clock, at no particular frame, so a view that animates through those is not stepped; one that sleeps on `Motion.Clock.Stepped` instead is. AVFoundation's reader has failed inside a process whose clock is frozen, so read a movie back (`Motion.Encoding.Inspection`, `read`) from another process. Measured on macOS 27.2 on Apple silicon; no other platform was tried.
- `Motion.Clock.Stepped` is the process's virtual clock as a clock of Swift's own, for code that waits: hand it to code that takes `any Clock<Duration>` or a generic clock, such as a model that blinks a cursor, and its waits are stepped with the frames and the same in every run. Its `now` is the process clock's `elapsed`, as an `Instant` from the freeze, and it counts whole nanoseconds. Making one freezes nothing: until a `Motion.Clock` is made it stands at zero, so a sleep begun before the freeze is measured from it, and it fails, as `Motion.Clock()` does, with `.absent` or `.unhooked` where the shim library is not hooked in from launch, so without the library there is no stepped clock at all. `sleep(until:tolerance:)` returns at once, without suspending, for a deadline the clock has reached, a sleep for zero among them; otherwise it suspends, holding no thread, until a `Motion.Clock` advance reaches the deadline, never by the wall clock, and with no advance nothing wakes. The sleeps an advance reaches are resumed one after another, earliest deadline first and, among equal ones, in the order they began: the advance resumes the first and returns, and each, as it resumes on the actor it slept on, resumes the next, so the sleeps of one actor run in that order, each up to its next suspension before the next begins. A sleeper reads `now` as the instant advanced to, which is its deadline when the advance stops there. A task cancelled before or while it sleeps throws `CancellationError` at once, the clock unmoved, and a later advance does not resume it. The tolerance is not used. For stepped time in code that hosts no view, where nothing else in the process must keep step, swift-clock's `Clock.Test` is the general clock and needs no shim; this one is the case whose time is the process's own, which the run loop's timers and the view's animations follow too.
- `Motion.Hosting.Live` hosts one view for a whole run of frames, where `Motion.Hosting.Window` gives each frame a fresh one: state, running animations and effects carry over. The view first runs on the machine's clock for `warm`, so what it does on appearing is over. Then the process's clock is frozen and the host's layer time is stopped.
- Each `frame` moves time on by one frame and draws the frame at `position`, in this order: the process's clock and the host's layer time go one frame's duration on, to an exact frame start counted from the freeze; in the same turn, before the run loop can draw anything at the new time, the caller's change is applied and the host lays the view out and draws it; the run loop gets `settle` of wall time, in which what the change set off elsewhere (a task, a stream, an effect) reaches the view; the host draws once more, so this frame shows it and not the next; the window is captured until a capture shows this frame's own fence and the next capture is the same picture; `position` goes up by one. The sleeps on `Motion.Clock.Stepped` whose deadlines the frame's time reaches begin to be resumed as time moves, before the change is applied; one on the main actor runs as soon as the frame's first turn ends, at the start of `settle`, so what it changes is drawn by the second draw and shows in this frame, and one on another actor reaches the view within `settle` as other work does. The frame at `position` p is drawn at the start of frame p + 1 counted from the freeze, so a sleep due at an instant wakes in the first frame drawn at or after it, the same frame in every run. So the view's own animations, SwiftUI's and Core Animation's alike, advance by exactly one frame's duration per frame and never with the wall clock, and the same run draws the same pictures every time.
- The order is the law's reason. A change that reaches SwiftUI after it has already drawn at the frame's time has its animations begin one frame late, which happened in about one run in six when time was moved after the capture; and an update SwiftUI puts off waits on a run-loop timer that a frozen clock never fires, so the host draws at once. Both kinds of animation read the host's layer time; the process's clock is what the run loop's timers and any code that reads uptime follow, and SwiftUI needs it moved to be woken at all. When an animation begins is the framework's own affair: Core Animation's begins at the frame of the change, SwiftUI's one frame after it.
- Work the change sets off that takes longer than `settle` to reach the view shows a frame late, and by the wall clock: `settle` is the one place wall time can still decide a picture, so give it room for the slowest such path.
- A `Motion.Take` is a recording: frames at a `Rate`, already drawn, whatever drew them, all the size of the first, its `width` and `height` (zero without frames); a frame of another size is refused with `.size` and its position. Each `Motion.Take.Frame` holds its `width`, `height` and `digest`, the SHA-256 of its width and height, eight bytes each little-endian, then its pixels' bytes in blue, green, red, alpha order, made once when the frame is made, so it is the same in every process. Two frames are equal and hash alike exactly when their digests are, which reads 32 bytes and never the pixels. A frame made from a raster holds it, and `held` gives it; `raster()` gives the pixels of any frame, held or read. `position(at:outside:)` is `index(at:)` of an instant at the take's own rate, as `Motion.Reel.frames(at:)` reads a reel; before zero and from the end of the last frame on it is what a `Motion.Take.Edge` says, nil for `.empty`, the default, and the first frame before and the last after for `.extend`, the nearest frame held, as `Raster.Edge.extend` holds the nearest pixel. A take of no frames shows none at any instant. `frame(at:outside:)` is that frame. Neither reads a pixel.
- `Motion.Hosting.Live.take(_:_:)` records the next frames by `frame(_:)`, as many as asked, with one change, into a take at the host's rate held in memory; a frame that fails fails the take. To record without holding the take, append each `frame(_:)` to a `Motion.Take.Recorder`.
- A take's written form, `Motion.Take.Written`, version 1, every number unsigned and little-endian: a 44-byte head (the eight bytes of `signature`, the version in four, the rate's numerator and denominator in lowest terms, the width and the height in eight each), then each distinct frame's pixels, premultiplied BGRA row after row as a `Motion.Pixel` holds them, compressed by DEFLATE (RFC 1951) at its fast level, then the index (the number of frames, and for each frame in order where its compressed pixels begin, how many bytes they are and its digest), then a 48-byte foot (where the index begins, the SHA-256 of the head and the index, and the signature again). It is exact, value for value: a take written and read back is equal to it, with the same rate, size, digests and rasters, pixel for pixel. A frame equal to one before it is written once and named again in the index, and its pixels are not read. PNG is not this form, since PNG holds straight alpha and these pixels are premultiplied. A rate whose numerator or denominator does not fit 64 bits is refused with `.rate` before anything is written.
- `Motion.Take.Writer` writes the form a frame at a time: `append(_:)` gives the bytes to add after those before (the head with the first frame), `tail()` the index and the foot, and `take(reading:)` the take the bytes hold without reading them back; it holds 48 bytes a frame and no pixels. `written()` is a whole take's form as bytes.
- `Motion.Take(length:reading:)` reads a take through a function that gives up to a count of bytes from an offset, fewer only where the bytes end, and `init(written:)` from bytes in memory. It reads and checks the head, the foot and the index and no frame's pixels. Bytes that do not begin with the signature fail with `.foreign`, another version with `.version` and that version, bytes cut short anywhere after the signature with `.truncated` (the foot's signature is missing), and a head or index that fails its SHA-256 or names bytes outside the frames' with `.index`. Each frame's `raster()` then reads that frame's bytes, and no other bytes, every time it is asked, inflates them, makes the raster, and checks it against the digest from the index; a frame that does not inflate, inflates to another size, holds a pixel brighter than its alpha or has another digest fails with `.damaged` and its position, and bytes that end before the frame's with `.truncated`. No damage is silent, and one damaged frame leaves the others readable.
- What a take read from its written form holds: its rate and size, and for each frame its size, its digest and where its bytes are (about a hundred bytes a frame), never pixels. A frame's raster lives as long as the caller that asked for it holds it; the take keeps none. `raster()` reads on the calling thread and may block, so a reel's frames, drawn on the threads of `Motion.Hosting.Picture.Frames`, read there, and a task on the cooperative pool should not call it.
- `Motion Take File` keeps a take in a file through swift-file-system, in a target of its own so `Motion`, which every other target uses, does not take on the file system. `Motion.Take(reading:)` of a `File.Path` reads the head, the index and the foot now and each frame's bytes, by `File.System.Read.Range`, when its `raster()` is asked; a file that cannot be read fails with `.unreadable` and the file system's reason, and a file replaced or cut short after it was opened fails when a frame is read. `Motion.Take.Recorder(to:rate:)` writes a take as it is recorded, each frame's bytes to the file when it is appended, through `File.System.Write.Streaming` into a hidden file beside the path that `finish()` renames into place, replacing a file there, so a reader sees no file or a whole one; a recorder let go of unfinished removes what it wrote. `finish()` gives the take as read from the file. `write(to:)` of a take is a recorder over its frames. Its failures are `Recorder.Error`: `.take` with the take's refusal, `.file` with the file system's, and `.finished` for a recorder already finished.
- `Motion.Hosting.Picture` of `Motion Picture` draws a `Picture` of swift-picture, where the other hosts draw a view: `raster(_:width:height:over:)` asks the renderer in the current `Witness.Context`, which is `Picture.Renderer.cpu` unless `withWitnesses` set another, to draw the picture over a background, black unless given, and turns the result into a frame. A picture's point (x, y) is the frame's position (x, y), the first row on top. It needs no window, screen or permission and holds no state, so frames can be drawn on any thread and in any order. A renderer's failure is thrown as it is, and equal pictures give equal frames.
- `Motion.Hosting.Picture.Frames` draws many frames of pictures at once: it holds a size, a background, a renderer and a function from a frame's position to its picture, and a `Kernel.Thread.Pool` of swift-threads with as many threads as it has `workers`, by default one for each logical processor of the machine; that pool's own default of at most four threads is not used. Each thread draws one frame at a time with one thread inside the frame (the renderer is `Picture.Renderer.cpu(threads: 1)` unless another is given, never the one in the `Witness.Context`), and takes the next position when it is done, so a dear frame holds up no other and a slower core simply draws fewer; nothing tells performance cores from efficiency cores. `drawn(_:as:)` draws the frames at some positions and gives back what a function made of each working raster, on the thread that drew it, in the order of the positions; `rasters(_:)` is that with the movie's pixels. They are the frames `raster(_:width:height:over:)` draws one by one through the same renderer, pixel for pixel. All frames go through the one renderer value, so what it keeps for an image that names its picture is drawn once for every frame that shows it. A `Frames` holds its pool's threads, as many as its workers, from when it is made until the last copy of it, and of every `Feed` made from it, is let go, whether it drew or not; then they end, with nothing to call. It is a value to hold and draw from many times: each one made, as each call of a reel's `hosting(_:workers:renderer:)` makes one, is a new set of threads.
- `sheet(_:columns:gap:fill:)` is a contact sheet: each frame made `columns` times as small along each side by `Raster.resampled` with the box filter, the sides rounded down, and tiled by `Raster(tiling:columns:gap:fill:)`. `feed(first:ahead:as:)` is a `Feed` for frames asked for one after another: asked for a frame it does not hold, it draws that frame and the ones after it, `ahead` of them or four for each worker, side by side, and lets go of the stretch before; from the count on it gives nil. `feed(first:ahead:)` feeds the movie's pixels, so `Motion.Encoding.write(frames:to:raster:)` is fed by `{ try? await feed.frame(at: $0) }`, and a loop over the positions that writes what `frame(at:)` gives is a numbered sequence of stills.
- A frame the renderer refuses fails the run with `Motion.Hosting.Picture.Frames.Error.renderer` and the frame's position, the renderer's error as it came, a drawing's own failure among them, and a frame whose picture could not be built, its words not set, with `.building` and the position; either way no further frame is started; the pool's refusal is `.pool`, and frames `Raster(tiling:columns:gap:fill:)` refuses are `.sheet` with its error.
- `Motion.Pairs` of `Motion Picture` sets two films side by side at the same instants: each `Pair` is an instant, a reference frame on the left and another film's frame on the right, either nil where the film shows nothing. `sheet(_:)` makes each frame `width` by `height` by `Raster.resampled` with the box filter in linear light, so a frame of another shape is stretched to the tile, sets a missing one as a tile of `fill`, lays the two frames `gap` apart, and tiles the pairs `columns` to a row by `Raster(tiling:columns:gap:fill:)`; `png(_:)` is that sheet as PNG bytes. `instants(every:before:count:)` is every step from zero before an end, in sheets of a count. The frames come from wherever the caller reads them: `Motion.Decoding.frames(at:)` for a movie and `Motion.Reel.frames(at:of:workers:renderer:)` for a reel drawn directly, which draws the frame at `rate.index(at:)` of each instant through `hosting(_:workers:renderer:)` and gives nil before zero and from the end of its frames on. The library is the layout; the decoding is the Apple glue's.
- A `Motion.Pairs.Label` writes each pair's instant above it, in seconds with two decimals, the hundredths rounded to the nearest, halves up, in a row the label's size and a quarter tall, through swift-picture's `Picture.Text` in a font whose file the caller gives. This package bundles no font: without a label there is no row. A font that does not parse fails with `.label`.
- `Motion.Graphics` of `Motion Graphics` holds components for a motion picture's screen, every one the same shape: a value that holds what it shows and where (its words, its place in the frame), a `Style` that holds every decision of its look, with no default look, and a `Moment`, its progress values at one instant, mostly 0 to 1, at which `Picture.init(_:at:)` draws it. A component keeps no time: a caller's timeline, such as a `Timeline.Track` of swift-timeline, makes the moments. Every moment's `hidden` draws the empty picture, and `resting` draws the component whole and in place (a list's `resting(_:)` takes how many lines, rows or letters); a list's moment holds one moment for each of its lines or rows, and one without a moment is hidden.
- A component that sets type sets it once, when it is made: its init fails with swift-picture's `Picture.Text.Error` when a font cannot set its words, and drawing never fails and sets no type. A typed share shows the first glyphs of what was set, counted as `Picture.Text.length(of:)` counts them, one for each unicode scalar but a line feed. A component whose content changes as it plays takes the change in its moment: a readout's count is `Readout.Moment.count`.
- The components are built from a few shared parts. A `Motion.Graphics.Ink` is how type is set: a parsed `ISO_14496_22.FontFile`, a size, a colour and a tracking. A `Motion.Graphics.Glow` is swift-picture's `Picture.glow(deviation:strength:sparing:)`: a deviation, a strength at rest and the strength a full flash adds, so at a moment's flash f it glows at `strength + flash × f` (a readout adds its heat before the flash); every component's glow is optional, nil drawing none. A `Motion.Graphics.Slot` is the window a line of type rises through, from `above` sizes over its resting baseline to `below` sizes under it. A `Motion.Graphics.Plate` is what a component stands on: a `Surface` inside `Corners`, then, with a coat or an edge, one shape of the outline filled with the coat, a gradient from the plate's top to its foot, and stroked with the edge's pen; where it stands, its insets and its baselines are the component's. A point a component stands at is its `place`, given `at:`, with an `anchor` where the point is a line's left end, middle or right end; a rectangle it fills is its `frame`, given `in:`.
- Corners are `circular` (quarter circles of a radius), `continuous` (quarters of a superellipse of a radius and an exponent, about 5 for the continuous corner of industrial design) or a `capsule`; `region(_:)` is the plate's `Picture.Plane.Region` and `path(_:)` its outline, a continuous corner flattened to within `Corners.tolerance` by swift-geometry, every vertex on the region's boundary. A surface is a `fill` of the outline; `frosted`, what is behind blurred under a coat, which is a `Picture.Glass.Pane` of the region with that frost and that tint and no rim or thickness; or a pane of a `Picture.Glass` with a thickness and a rim. `elements(_:corners:)` draws it; a clear fill draws nothing. A pane reads the picture beneath it wherever it stands, inside a layer too, so a plate of glass fades with its component as a fill does.
- `Motion.Graphics.Window` is a window's chrome: its plate round its frame, a title bar with its traffic lights and its title centered at the middle of its capitals, and a rule under the bar; `body` is the frame below the bar and its rule. Its moment fades it.
- `Motion.Graphics.Transcript` sets terminal lines in cells of one advance, the typed ink's pitch when its font is fixed pitch and otherwise its capital M's advance, each line's first baseline its place's lowered by `leading` for every row before it. A line's mark (a prompt, or a label in its own colour) and one space take its first cells, and every row of its words starts after them; the words take the typed ink after a prompt and the said ink otherwise. A line feed starts a row, and with `columns` a row breaks before a word that would end beyond them, the spaces at the break counted among the glyphs but shown on no row. At a typed share s of a line's n glyphs (its `length`), its first k glyphs stand, the most k with k / n at most s, which is floor(s · n) exactly, so a share made as k / n shows k; the caret is a block in the cell after the last of them, on the row where the next glyph would go, glowing by its glow.
- `Motion.Graphics.Typing` is the instants of a hand's strokes, a `Timeline.Series` of swift-timeline made by `Timeline.instants(_:from:to:weights:)` of a count between a first and a last stroke: `standing(at:)` is the series' `count(at:)`, how many fall at or before an instant, `share(at:)` that over the count, and `lit(at:blink:)` whether a caret is lit, always while typing and otherwise a loop of one blink on and one off, from instant zero before the first stroke and from the last stroke after it. `Typing(_:key:cadence:from:to:)` types a text in a `Typing.Cadence`: one stroke for each glyph, the gap before glyph i weighted by a `Timeline.Jitter` of the cadence's spread under the key at i, `1 + spread × (2s − 1024) / 1024` for s the top ten bits of the key's Threefry block, times `word` when it begins a word and `space` when it is a space. The instants are a pure function of the text, the key, the cadence and the two ends, so one key types alike in every run, and they never go back; a spread outside 0…1 traps, as the jitter's does.
- `Motion.Graphics.Caption` is one transcript line on a plate, its last baseline through its place, which is the line's left end, middle or right end as its anchor says. The plate is sized for the whole line however much of it is typed: from the style's ascent above the first baseline to its descent below the last, the line's width across, padded by its insets. Its moment's `shown` fades it in and out and `risen` lifts it into place from `rise` below.
- `Motion.Graphics.Headline` sets its lines once in its ink, at one size, the size at which the widest is as wide as the measure and at most the ink's size, `pitch` sizes apart and centered as a block on its place; as the block opens, the first line goes from standing alone, its capitals centered, to its place in the block. A line's `offset` is where it stands across from the center, and its moment's `drift` moves it across from there at an instant, so lines that drift apart over time need no group of their own; a drift of 0 draws the line where it stands. Each line rises from `rise` sizes below its place and goes `rise` sizes above it, clipped to its slot `span` wide, and inside the region its letters take the second colour. `placements(at:)` is where each line stands.
- `Motion.Graphics.Checkbox` is a box that springs in to at most `overshoot` of its size, its corners growing with it up to their radius, a fill that grows from its middle, never under its least half, and glows by its glow at the flash, and a tick through points about its place, trimmed to the share drawn. `Motion.Graphics.Checklist` is a heading, a statement that rises inside its slot across the plate, and a `Row` for each item, the first on the layout's first baseline and each next one a pitch lower; a row is a box whose center stands its rise above the baseline, the name a gap right of the box's center sliding in and fading by `named`, and the typed share of the tag's glyphs, ending where the whole tag ends at the row's right edge. A row stands alone as well. The plate grows about its middle by `settle` as it settles, its coat running over the grown plate, and fades as it shows.
- `Motion.Graphics.Readout` is a label, a count, a word and a total, set as one line in tabular figures (proportional figures are set tabular) so the count's digits share one advance and the plate, the word and the total stand still whatever it reads. The line is set once with the count's place filled with zeros as wide as the total's digits, and the moment's count is laid digit by digit from the ten digits set once in the same figures, padded with zeros by swift-formatter's `Formatter.Number`; a count above the total reads as the total. A bar under them is filled by the count's share of the total as the readout grows in. Its accent mixes the style's colours as written, by heat, then toward its finish; the edge, which a readout draws in the accent at its edge alpha whatever the plate's edge colour, the count, the bar and the glow take the accent. It grows in about the plate's middle and fades `fade` times faster than it grows, times its light, which dims it without shrinking it. `plate` is the plate's rectangle at rest, the same whatever the count reads, and `transform(at:)` the growth the whole readout is drawn under at a moment, so the plate at a moment is `plate` under it: a caller lays something of its own on the plate, or turns and drops the readout in a group of its own, without setting the line again.
- `Motion.Graphics.Title` is a line of runs set once in its ink as one line, when the title is made (`text` is that line), each run taking the style's run look in turn: a colour (the ink's without one), a rise, a growth, a fade and a floor. Its moment holds how far each glyph has entered; at e a letter stands `(1 − e) × rise` sizes below its place, `1 + grow × (1 − e)` times its size about the middle of its ink across and of its capitals down, at an opacity of `fade × e`, at least the run's `floor` (0 by default) and at most 1. At 1 every letter stands where the line placed at the title's place stands, past 1 a spring's overshoot carries it beyond, and a glyph without ink or at 0 is not drawn. `letters(at:)` gives each letter's glyph, transform, colour and opacity, and so the outlines of the landed letters, without setting the line again.
- `Motion.Graphics.Spinner` is a progress ring: a track round its place, glowing by the track's own glow, and over it an arc from the top turned by its moment's `turn`, clockwise on a screen, over the share `filled`, glowing by the arc's glow; both glow at their strength at the moment's flash and spare the spinner's `mask`, a picture in the spinner's space whose alpha can be soft, or without one the disc inside the ring (`spared`). A track without a glow is a plain stroke under the arc. A share of 1 or more is the whole ring, a closed circle, which is its `resting` moment; held at a share while it turns, it is a spinner that does not know how long it will take.
- `Motion.Graphics.Shockwave` is a ring of light thrown from a point, with echoes behind it when it has a trail. At a moment s the lead ring is at its own spread s × (1 + count × lag) and echo k k lags behind it; a ring of spread p is drawn while p is from 0 up to 1, not including 1, at a radius of `radius + reach × e(p)` for e the style's `travel` easing (linear by default), its pen going from `pen` to `least`, its softness, the deviation of a Gaussian blur, from `soft` to `softened` (0, a hard stroke, by default), and its colour toward `finish` by p itself, its `light` the style's `fall` easing at 1 − p (falling evenly by default) times the trail's fade once a step behind, and its colour faded by that light, so each ring's light falls to nothing as it ends. At 0 the wave is just thrown: its starting ring at full light, which from a starting radius of 0 is a point of light, a disc the pen wide. Below 0, `hidden`, it has not set out, and from 1 on it is gone: both draw nothing. `light(at:moment:)` is how brightly the passing wave lights a thing at a distance from its center, the brightest of its rings' `Ring.light(at:)`: a ring's light at its radius, falling evenly to nothing a pen to either side, its softness not counted, so a caller flashes what the wave passes by the law the rings are drawn with. The rings glow with the style's glow and add their light; with no glow they are drawn without one.
- `Motion.Graphics.Marquee` fills its frame with rows a pitch apart, row i repeating the `i mod n`th of its lines, set once, a unit apart (the words' width and the gap), filled or outlined as the style's faces say in turn. `offset(row:travel:)` is how far left of the frame the row's first copy starts, from 0 up to the unit: it wraps every unit, even rows slide left and odd rows right as the travel grows, and the copies always reach past the frame's right edge. Each row's own travel is the shared travel plus its start, the style's `starts` taken in turn (none by default). Each row rises into place by its moment, from the style's `rise` below (a pitch without one), and is clipped inside the frame to its slot about its resting baseline, or to its band, a pitch tall, without one.
- `Motion.Graphics.Glint` is a band of light that sweeps once across a shape, slanted from upright, clear at its sides and its colour at its middle, filling only the shape, glowing by its glow and adding its light; its moment's `light` dims it. Its middle travels evenly from half a band before the shape's box to half a band past it, so at 0 and 1 it lies on no point of the shape and draws nothing. `Motion.Graphics.Twinkle` is a brief star at its place: rays through it and a glow at its core, at `sin(π × shone)` of their length, width and light, turning by `turn` over the twinkle, so nothing at its start and end and whole halfway; the core's radius is its `base` (0 by default) plus `radius` times that shine. These light effects keep to Motion Graphics because what makes them is the moment; the light they draw is swift-picture's glow and gradients.
- `Picture.Color.init(_:)` of a `Motion.Pixel` is the colour the pixel writes: each colour level divided by alpha as an sRGB-encoded value, and alpha over 255; a pixel of no alpha is `clear`.
- `Motion.Pixel.init(linear:green:blue:alpha:)` is the pixel nearest to a colour in linear light whose red, green and blue are already multiplied by alpha. Alpha is held to 0…1 and becomes one of 256 levels, rounded to the nearest, halves up. Each colour is divided by alpha, held to 0…1, encoded with the sRGB transfer function and becomes one of 256 levels as if opaque; that level is multiplied by alpha's level over 255, rounded the same way. So an opaque colour is exactly its sRGB level, a full colour under any alpha is exactly that alpha's level, light brighter than white is white, what is not a number is nothing, and no colour comes out above its alpha.
- No wait of a host is without an end. ScreenCaptureKit is asked what can be captured, and for each capture, with a limit of ten seconds on the wall clock: past it `Motion.Hosting.Window` gives nil, and `Motion.Hosting.Live` fails the frame with `.capture` (or `.window` while starting) and takes no further capture for it. The call that did not answer is left behind, not stopped. A wait of no time returns in the same turn, and a wait whose task is cancelled ends at once, so a time limit on the caller's side does end a frame that is waiting.
- A frame that never shows its fence fails with `.capture`, one whose captures never agree with `.unsettled`, a size that is not whole points on the main screen or is narrower than 16 points with `.size`, and a missing clock with `.clock`. A failed frame leaves `position` where it was, though time has moved. `close()` closes the window and leaves the clock frozen. `Motion.Hosting.Live` needs Screen Recording permission, one capturing process at a time, and waits `settle` in wall time, since a wait measured on uptime would be measured on the frozen clock.
- A `Motion.Reel` is an edit: parts played one after another at one `Rate`, at a size, over a background, with layers laid over spans of the whole. A `Motion.Reel.Part` has a key the caller chooses, which any number of parts may share, a number of frames, and a function from a `Motion.Reel.Frame` of its own, its position counted from zero at its start and the instant at that frame's center, to a `Picture`. `Part(_:scene:picture:)` makes one from a `Motion.Scene` and a drawing of its state at each frame's center. A part made with `building:`, `Part(_:frames:building:)` or `Part(_:scene:building:)`, builds what draws it the first time one of its frames is drawn, once, whichever thread draws it, and never before: a reel's length, places and positions build nothing, and drawing the frames of one part builds that part alone, so a reel of costly scenes pays only for the scenes it draws. The building may fail with swift-picture's `Picture.Text.Error`, as a component's init does when its words do not set: it is still called once, and every frame of that part then fails, `Part.picture(_:)` and the reel's `picture(at:)` with `Motion.Reel.Error.building` of the part's key and the failure, frames drawn through `hosting` with `Motion.Hosting.Picture.Frames.Error.building` and the position, which the reel's `failure(_:of:)` turns back into the part's key, as proofs and `Motion.Command` do. The parts are a `Timeline.Stack.sequence` of swift-timeline with no overlap; a part of no frames is not in it and shows nowhere.
- A reel lasts `frames`, the sum of its parts' frames. `place(of:)` looks a frame's center up in the sequence and gives the part it falls in, by its place among the parts and its key, and the part's own frame, the index of its age there: a part's first frame is zero, the first frame after a cut is the next part's zero, and from `frames` on it is nil. This is exact at any rate, 30000/1001 included.
- A reel's layer is a `Motion.Reel.Layer`: a key of the reel's key type, which any number of layers may share, and a `Timeline.Layer` whose lifetime is a span of the reel's time in seconds and whose content draws a frame of the reel, by the reel's position and center. `picture(at:)` is the part's picture of its own frame with the picture of each layer alive at the frame's center laid over it, in the order of the layers, so a layer is drawn on the frames whose center is in its lifetime, its end not included; from `frames` on the picture is empty.
- `positions(during:)` is the reel's frames whose center falls in a span of its time, the end not included, so `positions(ofLayer:)` is the frames the layers with a key are drawn on, in order, each once. `layered(_:)` is the same reel with only the layers whose keys are given, in the reel's order, and with no keys, none.
- `positions(of:)` is the reel's frames of the parts with some keys, every part with each key in the reel's order, the keys in the order given, or every frame without keys. `hosting(_:workers:renderer:)` is a `Motion.Hosting.Picture.Frames` at the reel's size and background whose frame `i` is the `i`th of those positions as `picture(at:)` draws it, so a part drawn alone keeps the layers it has in the whole reel, and the frames are the parts' own pictures drawn one by one, pixel for pixel. Past the last position the frame is the background.
- Proofs of a reel are counted among the frames printed: those of the parts chosen, played one after another, so for one part the part's own frames, and for no parts chosen every frame from the reel's start. A `Motion.Reel.Proof` holds `Motion.Reel.Sheet`s, each a name, frames, a number of columns, a gap and its fill; stills, frames wanted at full size; and pictures by name that are no frame of the reel, such as a component shown alone, each drawn by itself at the reel's size over its background through the same renderer and given in `Prints.pictures` as a PNG. `prints(_:of:workers:renderer:)` draws every frame on a sheet or among the stills once, and gives each sheet under its name as the PNG of the raster `Motion.Hosting.Picture.Frames.sheet(_:columns:gap:fill:)` makes of its frames, and each still as the PNG of the frame, through `W3C_PNG.Image` of swift-picture's PNG integration, compressed fast. A frame beyond the frames printed fails with `.outside` before anything is drawn. `Motion.Reel.Proof.overview(of:)` is one unnamed sheet of every frame, or every so many as keep it to at most sixty, ten to a row, and the first, middle and last frames; `overview(of:)` of a list of frames does the same over those frames, such as a layer's.
- `scaled(by:)` is the same reel drawn at a scale: every picture, a part's, a layer's and a proof's picture alike, is drawn under a scale about the top left corner, and the width and height are multiplied and rounded to the nearest pixel, halves up, never below one, so a movie or a proof of a scaled reel is at the scaled size. `scale` says the factor, 1 unless scaled. At 0.5 the frame is half as wide and high, about a quarter of the drawing, and close to the full frame made smaller with the box filter: only the partly covered pixels of edges differ, and the tests hold it above 40 dB PSNR (an error under one percent of the range; 59 to 64 dB measured on slanted edges). A factor that is not a positive finite number, and 1, give the reel as it is.
- `sequence(_:ahead:workers:renderer:)` is a `Feed` of every chosen frame as a PNG, numbered from zero, each made on the thread that drew it; `movie(_:ahead:workers:renderer:)` is how many frames there are and a `Feed` of their movie pixels, which `Motion.Encoding.write(frames:to:raster:)` asks in order. The library makes bytes and writes no file.
- A take's frame is a picture through swift-picture's own image. `Raster<Picture.Pixel>.init(_:)` of a frame's raster makes each pixel a working pixel as `Picture.Pixel.init(_:)` of a `Motion.Pixel` does, and is undone exactly: every `Motion.Pixel` made a working pixel and back by `Motion.Pixel.init(_:)` is itself, at every alpha. `Motion.Take.Frame` is a `Picture.Image.Drawing` whose raster is that of `raster()`, read when a renderer first asks for it and drawn through no renderer, and a `Swift.Mirror.Written` that writes its size and digest and never its pixels, nor where they are, so a picture showing a frame is compared, hashed and keyed in a `Picture.Store` by the digest: equal frames from any two takes, in any process, give equal pictures and one stored entry, one differing pixel another, and a renderer turns an equal frame into working pixels once for all the pictures that keep it. A frame whose pixels cannot be read fails the renderer with `.drawing` of a `Picture.Image.Failure` holding the take's own `Motion.Take.Error`, the one `raster()` gives, which is why that error is `Hashable`: a damaged frame drawn in a reel fails the frames with `.renderer` of that failure at the frame's position, and the reel's `failure(_:of:)`, proofs and `Motion.Command` carry it as `.frames`, unchanged, so `error(as: Motion.Take.Error.self)` of the failure is `.damaged` with the frame's place in its take. What the renderer keeps of a take's frames is bounded by the renderer, not by the take: `Picture.Renderer.cpu(threads:)` keeps at most 2²⁵ working pixels of drawings, 16 bytes each (512 MiB, about 36 frames of 1280 × 720), the ones asked for longest ago going first, so a reel showing hundreds of a take's frames holds no more than that, and `cpu(threads:keeping:)` with fewer pixels keeps less. Measured on macOS 27.2 on Apple silicon, drawing 300 distinct frames of a 1280 × 720 take on eight workers: the cache held 510 MiB in 37 blocks after the run, the same after a second run, and each worker about 46 MiB while drawing; with nothing kept, about 24 MiB stayed live. The system's allocator keeps freed large blocks (330 to 450 MiB here) until memory is short, which the process's footprint counts.
- `Picture.Image(frame, placement:sampling:edge:)`, swift-picture's own init for a drawing, is the frame as an image naming it, under an affine or projective placement, the identity putting its first pixel's corner at the origin and one pixel a unit, as any raster image stands. `Picture(_:placement:sampling:edge:)` of a frame is the quadrilateral its corners go to under the placement, filled with that image, so it composes under groups, layers, effects and glass like any picture. `Picture(_:at:outside:placement:sampling:edge:)` of a take is that picture of `frame(at:outside:)` of an instant and the empty picture where no frame is shown, so outside the frames the empty picture by default and the first or last frame held for `.extend`, so a part or a layer shows a take by asking at its frame's `time`: at the reel's rate frame k shows the take's frame k, and at another rate the frame whose span holds the reel frame's center. At the identity an opaque take's frames drawn as a reel's part come back pixel for pixel.
- `Motion.Command` is a film's command line, and `Motion Command` re-exports `Motion`, `Motion Picture`, swift-picture's `Picture` and swift-timeline's `Timeline`, and `Motion Encoding` where AVFoundation is, so a film's executable imports it alone. A `Motion.Command.Film` is a reel, proofs of its parts by key, and proofs of the whole reel by name. `main(arguments:_:)` reads the arguments, or the process's without the executable's name, makes the film from the request with the function given, so a value such as an assets directory reaches the maker as `--set <name>=<value>` and `Request.value(_:)`, writes, prints how many files it wrote, and ends the process with 0; it ends with 1 when the maker throws, printing its error. `main(_:arguments:)` takes a film made beforehand, and `main(_:proofs:arguments:)` a reel and its parts' proofs; it prints the help and ends with 0 for `--help`, prints the reason and ends with the code of swift-arguments' `Command.Diagnostic` when the arguments cannot be read, and ends with 1 when writing fails. `Motion.Command.Request.parse(_:)` and `run(_:_:proofs:)` are the same without ending the process. A request is `sheet` or `frames`, and where AVFoundation is `movie` or `compare`, a directory, made if missing, and `--part` names, given one by one or joined by commas; a part is named by `String(describing:)` of its key, and a name no part has is refused with `.part` before anything is written.
- Every action draws the reel with only the layers `--layer` names, by `String(describing:)` of their keys, given one by one or joined by commas, or with none for `--bare`; a name no layer has is refused with `.layer`. Without AVFoundation, as on Linux, the command builds without `movie`, `compare` and the movie-file command line, and `sheet` and `frames` work the same.
- Every action takes `--scale <factor>`, a decimal above zero, and draws `scaled(by:)` of the reel, so `--scale 0.5` is a draft at half the size; and `--cache <directory>`, which keeps what each action makes of a frame in a `Picture.Store` of swift-picture's `Picture Store` for the frames' own renderer, `Picture.Renderer.cpu(threads: 1)`: a frame whose picture, size, background, drawing edition and form were met before, by this run or another, is read back, and any other is drawn and kept. A run through a warm cache writes the same files as a run without one, a draft and the full size never share an entry, and a damaged entry is drawn again; several runs may share a directory at once. Nothing is ever removed from it: delete the directory to free its space. The cache is for iteration; a drawing of record runs without it.
- `Motion.Hosting.Picture.Frames` with a `store` looks each frame up before drawing it and keeps what it drew, by a `Form`: a name that is part of the key, what is made of the drawn raster, and how that is kept as bytes and read back, value for value. `Form.movie` keeps a movie's frame as its 8-bit pixels, 4 bytes a pixel (8.3 MB at 1920 by 1080); `Form.deflated` keeps the same bytes compressed by RFC 1951 at its fast level, under a name of its own, and reads back the same frame, value for value; `rasters(_:)` and `feed(first:ahead:)`, so a reel's movie and its frames for `compare`, keep frames `deflated`. On full-HD frames of gradients, slanted marks and grain it kept about a sixth of the bytes (1.4 MB) for about 30 ms more on a frame drawn and 3 to 15 ms more on a frame read, where drawing one took 160 to 330 ms on one thread. Either form checks every pixel it reads back to be premultiplied, since the store's Adler-32 finds damage by chance, not for certain; `Form.png` keeps the PNG a still or a numbered frame is written as; `Form.working(_:_:)` keeps a small raster of working pixels, as a sheet's tiles are. A proof keeps, for each frame it draws, its tiles and its still in one entry. A form without a name is never kept. The store's edition must be that of the frames' renderer.
- AVFoundation's writer does not write the same bytes twice for the same frames, so a movie through a warm cache is held to the same frames, not the same file.
- `sheet` with `--proof` names, one by one or joined by commas, writes those proofs of the whole reel, and a name the film has no proof of is refused with `.proof`. A proof named `<name>`, a part's by its key, is written as its sheets `sheet-<name>.png` or `sheet-<name>-<sheet>.png`, its stills `<name>-<frame>.png` and its pictures `<name>-<picture>.png`.
- `sheet` with `--at` frames or `--stills` frames, numbers with commas between, writes one sheet of the `--at` frames, `--columns` to a row (five unless given), as `sheet-<parts>-at.png`, and the stills as `<parts>-<frame>.png`, `<parts>` being the names joined by `+`, or `reel`. Without them it writes, for each part named or each key of the reel in the order of its parts, the proof the film gives for that key, or its overview, and when no part is named, every proof of the whole reel after them, by name. `frames` writes every chosen frame as `<frame>.png`; `movie` writes `<parts>.mp4` through `Motion.Encoding` at the reel's size and rate, replacing one there. `compare` with `--reference <movie>` sets the movie beside the chosen frames of the reel drawn directly, as the movie-file `compare` below does, until the chosen frames end; each pair's reference frame and label are at the reel's instant of its frame, by `Motion.Reel.instants(_:of:)`, so a part is compared with the movie where the part plays in it. Frame numbers have at least five digits. Files are written whole through swift-file-system, so a reader sees the old file or the new one.
- `Motion.Command.main(arguments:)` is the command line over movie files, which needs no reel, read as a `Motion.Command.Movies`: `compare <reference> <movie> <directory>` writes `pairs-<sheet>.png` sheets of `Motion.Pairs`, a pair every `--step` seconds (0.5 unless given) from zero until the longer movie ends, `--pairs` to a sheet (10), two to a row, each frame `--width` pixels wide (480) and as tall as keeps the reference's shape, labelled in the `--font` file given or not at all; `score <movie> <sound> <output>` lays one cue under the movie with `--offset`, `--at`, `--gain`, `--fade-in` and `--fade-out`; `reduce <movie> <output>` makes it smaller with `--height` 540, 720 (unless given) or 1080, or with `--bytes <count>` to at most that many bytes, which then decides. Seconds are a whole number, a decimal or a fraction such as `1001/30000`, never below zero. `score` and `reduce` replace a file at their output. It ends the process as `main(_:proofs:arguments:)` does.
